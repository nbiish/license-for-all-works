TRIBAL 8(a) (STANDARD and highly lucrative): SBA rules give Indian tribes, ANCs, and tribally owned entities special 8(a) BD treatment — 13 C.F.R. 124.109 — tribes are deemed socially disadvantaged; economic-disadvantage rules are relaxed for tribal entities; tribally owned firms can have multiple 8(a) subsidiaries and no dollar cap on sole-source direct awards (13 C.F.R. 124.109(b)); CRS R40855 documents ANC 8(a) contract dollars rising from ~$508M (FY2000) to ~$5.2B (FY2008), and popular reporting puts ANC federal contract revenue above $11B in 2021 (hstoday — treat the $11B figure as secondary-sourced). The 2025 SBA proposed rule would tighten 'potential for success' showings for tribally owned applicants (PilieroMazza alert) — evolving. HOW TRIBES HOLD WEALTH BEYOND PER CAPS (STANDARD mechanics): IGRA, 25 U.S.C. 2710(b)(2)(B), limits uses of net gaming revenue (government operations, economic development, charity, per caps); per-capita payments require a Secretarial-approved Revenue Allocation Plan under 25 U.S.C. 2710(b)(3) (25 CFR part 290; Interior final rule on RAP review) and are TAXABLE income to members (see Section 4). Tribes therefore channel wealth into: (a) tribal sovereign wealth / permanent trust funds — multi-generational investment funds seeded with gaming or settlement revenue, professionally managed (Aspen Institute has cited the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians as a model; the Chickasaw Nation runs one of the larger tribal funds per secondary commentary — fund SIZE figures are UNVERIFIED here); (b) wholly owned tribal corporations (e.g., Chickasaw Nation Industries) operating federal and commercial businesses; (c) land/resource holdings and self-governance compacts (P.L. 93-638) that convert federal program dollars into block grants; (d) tribally chartered community trusts and IGRA trusts (Rev. Proc. 2011-56 deferral structure) that hold per caps/minors' shares. Tribes also benefit from the tax toolkit above (7873, 139E GWE) and from sovereign immunity in contracting — that immunity is a genuine structural advantage over private family offices, but it is tribal-government specific and not replicable by non-tribal families. EXOTIC vs STANDARD: ANC-style diversified federal contracting + permanent funds is standard for large Alaska/tribal entities; 'tribal sovereign wealth fund' as an explicit SWF brand is newer and still maturing (FSA Investment Group marketing; academic/press coverage) — treat growth claims skeptically.
